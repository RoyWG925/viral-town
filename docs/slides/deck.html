AMD Developer Hackathon ACT III · Track 4

VIRAL

One whisper. A whole town reacts.

A butterfly-effect game where eight AI residents — Gemma on AMD — turn one sentence into an evening nobody scripted.

The problem

Stories either ignore your words or ignore consequences

Scripted stories

Branching trees are authored by hand: expensive, finite, and they only react to the choices the writer predicted.

Chatbot stories

Free text in, free text out — but nothing in the world changes, nothing is checked, and the story forgets what is true.

What players want

To say anything, and watch a living world take it seriously — believable people, real consequences, and a clear "this happened because you said that".

The experience

You are not a character. You are a thought that spreads.

  1. Pick a resident of Mistport and whisper one sentence — anything.
  2. Watch them understand it their way, retell it, check it, hide it, act on it.
  3. At 20:00 the Lantern Festival shows what your sentence did: lanterns, doves, crowds, protests, a mayor on a banana peel.
  4. Read the front page of The Mistport Gazette: the chain of who told whom, and what was really true.
Different input, different evening

Same town. Different sentence. Different night.

🚧 Stage sealed

To Molly: “I heard tonight's stage was thrown together from old planks.”

She phones the police chief, who finds a loose strut and tapes the stage off. Gus slips on a banana peel on his way to post “Everything is perfectly safe!”. The show moves to the open plaza.

📰 Budget exposé

To Rosa: “The stall fee went up because the mayor spent the money on a new car.”

No car — but the reporter checks the town hall accounts, finds a real 30% overrun and publishes. The mayor calls it “a misunderstanding”. No closing fireworks.

🤐 Nothing happens

To Barney: “Tonight's lanterns are cheap junk. One spark and they'll go up in flames.”

He worries — “but it's just one stranger” — and keeps it to himself. The festival goes on exactly as planned.

Every example is a real recorded Gemma run you can replay in the game. Some sentences go nowhere — the town does not have to care.

Quality and consistency

The model proposes. The game decides what is true.

  • Own knowledge only: each prompt holds one resident's memory, card and relationships.
  • Validated JSON: impossible actions, invented facts, leaked secrets and over-confident beliefs go back with the reason.
  • Evidence thresholds for big actions — a stranger's word cannot close the stage.
  • Traceable: every event logs its causes; the ending is a pure function of the log.
  • Moderation: jailbreaks play in character, swearing is masked, harmful content is refused before any model call.
  • 157 automated tests and 302 logged real-model stories (lab/runs).
Cost per experience

Less than a quarter of a cent per story

$0.0013

median cost of a full story (15–38 Gemma calls) as reported by the AMD Token Factory platform during the event; the most expensive of 92 full stories was $0.0020.

  • Replays of recorded runs cost nothing.
  • The engine, the ending and the town are deterministic code — the model is only called for residents' decisions.
  • A per-story decision budget bounds time and cost.
  • Outside the event, at an assumed list rate (US$0.50 per million input tokens, US$3 per million output, no cache discount): ~US$0.03–0.07 per full story.
